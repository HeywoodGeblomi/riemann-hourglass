# The hourglass picture

A vision, not a proof, for a minimal non-reducible equation that would locate the zeros of zeta

**Heywood Geblomi**

From the conversation Riemann Hypothesis Algebraic Solution, opened August 19, 2026, in the Grok project Riemann.

![The hourglass picture](hourglass_figure.png)

The figure (also saved as `hourglass_figure.svg`) is a picture of the vision, not a derivation. An entropic side and an unentropic side lock together at a narrow waist. Primes sit at the waist as gatekeepers. A dashed average-height line marks where a hoped-for minimal non-reducible equation would come from, and where that equation would also locate the non-trivial zeros. Dots on the same line stand for eigenvalues of a self-adjoint operator, the Hilbert-Polya echo named in the conversation.

## The idea

Two sides lock together in an hourglass: an entropic side above and an unentropic side below, joined at a narrow waist. The primes sit there as gatekeepers. The hope is a minimal non-reducible equation, derived from the average height of the two sides, that would also locate the non-trivial zeros of the zeta function. In the conversation, Grok treated this picture as a vision, not a proof.

## What the conversation stated

- Grok's opening finding: there is no algebraic solution, and the Riemann Hypothesis was still unsolved as of August 19, 2026.
- The only literature echo named was Hilbert-Polya: the zeros as eigenvalues of a self-adjoint operator.
- The completed zeta function was written \(\xi(s) = \frac{1}{2}\, s(s-1)\, \pi^{-s/2}\, \Gamma(s/2)\, \zeta(s)\).

## A later turn, kept separate

The thread later turned to Prym and discriminant-12 work: McMullen's S-shaped translation surface, residual-zero real multiplication, and Lyapunov growth along a Teichmuller stretch. That work was treated as a specialist calculator, not a Riemann Hypothesis machine.

## What to do next

- Formalize the average-height equation, with a precise meaning of height on each side and of the primes as gatekeepers.
- Check whether the Hilbert-Polya operator can be written down explicitly, so the dots on the waist are eigenvalues of a real operator, not only a likeness.
- Connect the 2:1 geodesic ratios (20 exact, known sum 6/7 with a related plus-or-minus 1/7 piece, and candidates 4/7 and 2/7) to the height measure, and say whether they inform the average or stay in the calculator.
- Keep the discriminant-12 Prym calculator in its own lane: use it to test that connection, and do not treat it as a proof of the hypothesis.
- Write a one-line claim that can fail: what would count as finding the equation, and what would count as missing it.
